Prioritization Analysis for Argentina - Visualizer User Guide

4 Description of Base Information

4.1 Study Area and Planning Units

The study area encompasses the entire continental surface of the Argentine Republic, including the province of Tierra del Fuego. The Malvinas Islands, Isla de los Estados, the South Atlantic Islands, and the Argentine Antarctic Territory are excluded from the analysis. As a result, the considered surface area is 2,780,050 km².

The mentioned area is converted into a raster layer, with a POSGAR 2007 projection, and a spatial resolution of 2 km. This results in 693,594 cells of approximately 4 km² each. Each cell is used as a Planning Unit (PU).

4.2 Protected Areas

The available database of Protected Areas at the national level (Administración de Parques Nacionales) is used, including national and provincial protected areas equivalent to categories I to VI in the World Database on Protected Areas (WDPA). These areas were projected onto the PU raster for analysis, including all polygons from the database and discarding overlaps. Areas categorized as ‘proposed’, ‘not applicable’, ‘not reported’, and biosphere reserves were excluded. In this way, 71,236 PUs belonging to the current network of protected areas were identified, representing 10.2% of the study area.

4.3 Urban Areas

Urban areas with high-intensity use are excluded from the prioritization analysis as they are not suitable for conservation management. To identify these areas, the Human Footprint Factor (Venter 2016) is used. Those PUs with a human footprint factor greater than 25% (the threshold for distinguishing cities, highways, and main roads) are considered urban areas unsuitable for inclusion among priority conservation areas. Using this method, 9,601 PUs were identified as urban areas.

4.4 Biomass Carbon Map

The amount of biomass is estimated from land cover maps (MapBiomas 2024) to identify forests, grasslands, and agricultural areas. The biomass content of each class is further adjusted according to biomass tables by climatic zone (Buchhorn 2019). Belowground carbon is estimated from aboveground biomass and root-to-shoot ratios obtained from the Guidelines for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories (IPCC 2019).

4.5 Clean Water Provision

To identify areas of greatest relevance for clean water provision in Argentina, the Relative Raw Water Provision Service (RRWPS) index for human consumption is used, following the Co$tingNature methodology (M. Mulligan 2015). This is an index that measures the volume of clean water (unimpacted by humans) available for downstream beneficiaries.

The amount of water in each PU is derived from the water balance (precipitation minus actual evapotranspiration) accumulated downstream. The potential water provision services for each PU are first calculated as the volume of clean water available from upstream. The human impact on water quality is quantified using the Human Footprint on Water Quality (HFWQ) index (Mulligan 2011). The potential water service volume is calculated as accumulated precipitation downstream minus actual evapotranspiration. The raw water service depends on the intensity of downstream use, measured as the normalized area of irrigation, the number of people, and the number of dams downstream. Therefore, the RRWPS is the product of the potential service and normalized downstream beneficiaries.

The RRWPS is high where the prevailing climate and land use generate large volumes of clean water that can be used (and reused) by a large number of beneficiaries. Not all water provides a service, only that which is used by people or dams.

4.6 Biodiversity Data

The best available and taxonomically updated data on species distribution in Argentina are used. These data included all threatened terrestrial mammals, birds, reptiles, and amphibians present in Argentina. Marine mammals (Pinnipedia and Cetacea) were not included.

We collected information on species distribution from two sources: distribution maps and point records. For reptiles and amphibians, distribution maps (or species range maps) were downloaded from the IUCN Red List database (IUCN 2019) and corrected based on expert opinion and collection data, while their conservation status was obtained from national assessments. For mammals, distribution maps were obtained from the National Red List (SAREM 2019). For birds, distribution maps were first obtained from Birdlife International and the Aves Argentinas database eBird Argentina.

A reclassification of the threat status was carried out, reviewing the categories assigned by the IUCN Red List (IUCN 2019) and adopting national criteria when deemed appropriate (e.g., species classified as Least Concern by the IUCN but with small populations or marginal distributions in Argentina). In total, 350 species of threatened tetrapods inhabiting Argentina were analyzed in this study (Section 9).

The geographic range of species varies in precision, so the range of each species was refined, from the Extent of Occurrence (EOO) to an adjusted range. Land cover maps (MapBiomas 2024) were used to remove from the original EOO habitats that experts considered unlikely for the species.

4.7 Ecoregions

Argentina has 15 terrestrial ecoregions that can be viewed in the viewer (Morello 2012). This information is used to divide the ranges of species that extend across more than one ecoregion. The resulting intersections are considered individually with their own conservation target. For example, the distribution range of the Ocelot (Leopardus pardalis) spans four ecoregions: Dry Chaco, Humid Chaco, Yungas, and Paraná Forest. Since this species is nationally classified as Vulnerable, the conservation target is 80% of its range, but instead of applying it to the total range, it is applied by ecoregion, i.e., the target is to protect 80% of the Ocelot’s range in the Dry Chaco, 80% of its range in the Humid Chaco, etc. In this way, intraspecific variation is captured, and the role of the species in each of the ecosystems it inhabits is preserved. By applying this separation into sub-ranges by ecoregion, the number of ranges increases from 350 to 621 sub-ranges with individualized targets.

4.8 Land Use and Land Cover

To classify the dominant land use and land cover type in each PU, the MapBiomas Argentina classification (MapBiomas 2024) is used. The annual land cover and land use maps of MapBiomas Argentina are produced from pixel-by-pixel classification of Landsat satellite images. The entire process is carried out on the Google Earth Engine platform. The resulting maps have a resolution of 30 m x 30 m. In the viewer, the dominant cover (i.e., the most frequent) in each 2 km x 2 km PU is displayed.
